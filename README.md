# DCNFIS---Deep-Convolutional-Neuro-Fuzzy-Inference-System

This Repository includes codes for Mojtaba Yeganejou's PhD Thesis, titled: Deep Neuro-Fuzzy Systems: A Large Scale Image Classification Perspective. It will be upadted to include codes for all chapters. 

Abstract

Deep learning is heart of modern artificial intelligence, and currently the state-of-the-art approach for many tasks. In the space of 20 years deep neural networks have been transformed from a curiosity to the emblematic technology of our time, with modern large foundation models incorporating hundreds of trillions of neurons. However, this level of complexity makes the model incomprehensible. The risk this poses is that humans might reject these models; it is human nature to distrust what we do not understand. The need for understanding of AI has spurred significant recent interest in explainable artificial intelligence, which seeks to make AI systems more transparent and understandable to human users. This is not a new field, but one which is fundamental the field of artificial intelligence. Model performance and model interpretability have both been highly desirable, and seemingly contradictory, characteristics throughout the decades-long history of AI. Indeed, this contradiction is often expressed as the \emph{accuracy-interpretability tradeoff}, and it remains a key bottleneck in modern AI systems. Fuzzy sets and logic have long been one route by which interpretability of an AI system may be improved, and so the goal in this thesis is to investigate novel designs for hybrids of fuzzy logic and deep learning, to achieve both high performance and explainability. Our core contribution is to design and evaluate a novel end-to-end trainable deep convolutional neuro-fuzzy inference system, and an integrated explanation mechanism. We replace the classifier component of a given convolutional neural network with a neuro-fuzzy system. The rules that extracted from this classifier will be the basis for our explanation mechanism. We first evaluate the accuracy of our model on small- and large- scale computer vision datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and the ImageNet) and show that our model performs as accurately as the regular CNNs it was built from (including LeNet, ResNets, Wide-ResNets, and Xception). Thus, we have a potentially more interpretable model, without losing accuracy. We also compare our model's performance with existing deep and shallow fuzzy models on these datasets, finding that our model outperforms all of them, and thus we introduce it as the state-of-the-art fuzzy classifier in computer vision. We then focus on the generating explanations by treating each fuzzy rule in the classifier (there is one rule for each output class) as a fuzzy cluster. We find the medoid example of the cluster and treat it as a representative of that class. We perform a saliency analysis on each of the class representatives; these together form our global explanation of the model's decisions. We evaluate these explanations on the Fashion-MNIST and Retina-OCT 2017 datasets. Notably, our model also achieves the best generalization performance in the literature on the latter dataset. During our evaluations, we found that in both datasets multiple real-world concepts would sometimes be grouped into a single class. These classes seem to consist of multiple disjuncts, and our medoid-based explanations do not capture these complexities. We propose a method based on nonlinear dimension reduction and clustering that could reliably split these classes into more homogenous sub-clusters, with the medoid of each being determined; these form a refined global explanation. We measure the fidelity of our explanations by creating an auxiliary model based on the extracted medoids, and comparing its predictions against the actual predictions of our model. We further validate our model on a real-world condition monitoring problem; fault diagnosis in underground power cables where five different cable fault conditions (and undamaged cables) are differentiated using our model. We show that our model is not only more accurate than existing shallow and deep models, but its explanations provide actionable insights. In another application of our model, we design an outlier detection and removal algorithm based on our explanation mechanism. By excluding the detected outliers from the training set, the model generalizes better (i.e. the out-of-sample error is reduced). We show that our selection of examples to remove is more effective than the current state-of-the-art algorithm. Finally, we study how our model's robustness could be enhanced via the Dropout technique. We design a hybrid Dropout for deep neuro-fuzzy systems that combines the standard Dropout and fuzzy-rule Dropout, and show that this approach significantly improves generalization of our model on three benchmark datasets.
